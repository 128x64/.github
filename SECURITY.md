# 128x64 Security Policy

128x64 takes the security of its software, services, and community seriously. We appreciate responsible security research and encourage individuals to report vulnerabilities privately and responsibly.

This policy applies to publicly released software and projects developed and maintained by 128x64.

## 1. Scope

This policy covers security vulnerabilities affecting publicly released 128x64 projects, including their publicly released software, applications, APIs, and associated components.

Private, unreleased, experimental, and internal projects are not included in the public scope of this policy.

A project being publicly accessible does not grant permission to test, scan, access, or interfere with its systems.

## 2. Reporting a Vulnerability

If you believe you have discovered a security vulnerability, please report it privately.

You may report vulnerabilities through either of the following methods:

**GitHub Private Vulnerability Reporting**

If enabled for the affected repository, use GitHub's Private Vulnerability Reporting feature to submit your report.

**Email**

Current security contact:

raphaelcvarghese@gmail.com

security@128x64.com

The future address should only be used once it has been officially activated and announced.

Please include as much relevant information as possible, such as:

- The affected project or repository.
- A description of the suspected vulnerability.
- The potential security impact.
- Relevant version information.
- A safe explanation or demonstration sufficient to help us understand and reproduce the issue.
- Your preferred contact information.

Do not include passwords, access tokens, private keys, personal information, or other sensitive data in your report unless specifically necessary and requested through an appropriate secure channel.

## 3. Response Expectations

128x64 aims to acknowledge security reports within 72 hours of receipt.

This is an acknowledgment target, not a guarantee of investigation completion or remediation within that period.

Further communication may be necessary while the report is reviewed, validated, and addressed.

## 4. Responsible Disclosure

Please do not publicly disclose a suspected vulnerability, proof of concept, exploit details, or other information that could enable exploitation before 128x64 has had a reasonable opportunity to investigate and address the issue.

Please coordinate public disclosure with 128x64.

We appreciate researchers who give us reasonable time to investigate and remediate reported issues.

## 5. Testing Authorization

**Explicit written authorization from 128x64 is required before conducting security testing against any 128x64-operated system or service.**

This includes vulnerability scanning, penetration testing, automated testing, access-control testing, and other security assessments.

Finding a vulnerability in a publicly released project does not automatically authorize further testing.

Do not:

- Access accounts, systems, or data that do not belong to you.
- Attempt to bypass authentication or authorization without written permission.
- Disrupt services or affect their availability.
- Modify, delete, or damage data.
- Conduct social engineering or phishing.
- Test third-party services or infrastructure without their authorization.
- Continue testing beyond the scope of an explicitly authorized assessment.

If you encounter potentially sensitive information accidentally, stop testing and report the discovery privately.

## 6. Researcher Recognition

128x64 appreciates individuals who responsibly identify and report security vulnerabilities.

With the researcher's permission, we may recognize their contribution in an appropriate security acknowledgments section or other official 128x64 publication.

Recognition is not guaranteed and does not imply financial compensation.

## 7. Good-Faith Reporting

We ask researchers to act responsibly, respect privacy, avoid unnecessary access to information, and report findings privately.

128x64 will review reports in good faith. This policy does not provide blanket authorization, legal immunity, or permission to conduct testing outside an explicitly approved scope.
